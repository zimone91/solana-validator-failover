# Audit 3 — v0.6.9 (post-failover symmetry), late June 2026

**Scope.** The v0.6.8 → v0.6.9 implementation diff: porting the holder self-fence into the
promoted standby (with a re-take lockout), hard-stop hardening, state persistence across monitor
restarts, a detection-only collision pager, and a fatal cross-node timing guard.

**Method.** Eleven parallel focus-area reviews, adversarial verification of every candidate
finding, and a lead auditor independently re-reading the safety-critical functions and re-running
the suite. Findings accepted only with a reproduced failing scenario or a proof of impossibility.

**Verdict: NO-GO for tag as submitted — while explicitly confirming the prime directive held**
(no double-sign vector; every defect failed toward unstaked/stop/page). The blockers were
availability regressions that defeated the feature's own purpose, plus a test-honesty gap:

1. **S-1 (High).** The standby's become-staked path did not reset self-fence state inherited from a
   *previous* tenure — a freshly promoted node could instantly self-fence on stale flags, tearing
   down a legitimate takeover and entering a 600 s re-take lockout with no active voter.
2. **S-2 (High).** The persisted frozen-slot baseline could not distinguish a validator *replaying
   after a reboot* from one that was genuinely isolated — a routine reboot of a staked holder could
   fabricate a demote. (The fix starts the stall clock fresh on the first post-restart read instead
   of backdating it.)
3. **S-3 (Med).** One suite used a construct that fails to *parse* on bash 3.2, so on the stated
   test platform the whole suite silently did not run and "35/35 green" was in fact 34/35. This
   produced the permanent parse-gate in `run_all.sh` and, later, the CI interpreter matrix.

Eleven further findings (Low/Info) covered strike-counter hygiene, a state-file migration guard
that matched by suffix instead of exact default, non-atomic state writes, and doc/comment
precision. All blockers were fixed with revert-controls before the tag; the live testnet gate then
re-validated the four headline scenarios end-to-end with an on-chain observer confirming zero
overlap.

**What this round taught us.** Both High findings lived in *seams* (promotion↔fence-arming,
restart↔persisted-state) that function-level tests with hand-primed fixtures are structurally
unable to see — the tester's beliefs about the other side of the seam were encoded into the
fixtures. That observation shaped the entire v0.7 validation strategy.
